# SAP ERP Implementation: Success vs. Failure
### A Comparative Case Study of Siemens Healthineers and Lidl

## Project Overview

This academic research project examines the contrasting outcomes of SAP Enterprise Resource Planning (ERP) implementations at Siemens Healthineers and Lidl.

The study explores how strategic planning, business process alignment, technical complexity, leadership, and organizational change management influence the success or failure of large-scale ERP transformation initiatives.

**Project Type:** Academic Research | Comparative Case Study  
**Academic Program:** Master of Science in Management Information Systems  
**Institution:** Northern Illinois University

## Research Objectives

- Examine the strategic and operational factors influencing SAP ERP implementation outcomes.
- Analyze the implementation strategies adopted by Siemens Healthineers and Lidl.
- Identify critical success factors and root causes of ERP implementation failure.
- Evaluate the importance of business process standardization, governance, and change management.
- Derive practical lessons for future enterprise digital transformation initiatives.

## Case Study 1: Siemens Healthineers — Successful SAP Implementation

Siemens Healthineers initiated Project P58 to establish an independent, standardized ERP platform following its separation from Siemens AG.

### Key Highlights

- Consolidation of approximately 12 legacy systems into a unified SAP environment.
- Global SAP rollout alongside an SAP S/4HANA upgrade.
- Wave-based deployment supported by centralized configuration and local implementation teams.
- Integration of approximately 75 satellite systems and more than 275 interfaces.
- S/4HANA upgrade completed eight months faster than a sequential implementation approach, while remaining under budget.
- Adoption of RISE with SAP to support continued cloud transformation.

### Critical Success Factors

- Strong executive sponsorship and strategic alignment.
- Effective collaboration between internal teams and implementation partners.
- Standardized business processes and phased deployment.
- Coordinated testing, planning, and risk management.
- Continuous improvement and technical modernization.

## Case Study 2: Lidl — Failed SAP Implementation

Lidl initiated its eLWIS ERP modernization program in 2011 to replace legacy merchandise-management systems and improve inventory visibility and operational efficiency.

Despite significant investment, the initiative was eventually abandoned.

### Key Findings

- Reported financial losses exceeding €500 million.
- Misalignment between Lidl's inventory valuation practices and SAP's standard processes.
- Extensive software customization increased technical complexity.
- Leadership changes weakened project continuity and governance.
- Insufficient change management and employee adoption challenges.
- Heavy reliance on external consultants reduced internal ownership.

## Comparative Analysis

| Evaluation Dimension | Siemens Healthineers | Lidl |
|---|---|---|
| Implementation outcome | Successful, with continuing global rollout | Abandoned implementation |
| Business process alignment | Standardized and harmonized processes | Strong preference for legacy processes |
| Implementation approach | Phased, wave-based deployment | Complex enterprise-wide initiative |
| Customization | Controlled through centralized templates | Extensive customization |
| Leadership and governance | Coordinated cross-functional leadership | Leadership turnover and strategic misalignment |
| Change management | Collaborative implementation and testing | Adoption and training challenges |
| Business impact | Improved efficiency and process transparency | Significant financial losses and delayed modernization |

## Key Research Insights

1. **Business process alignment:** ERP success depends on aligning organizational workflows with system capabilities.
2. **Customization management:** Excessive customization increases technical debt, maintenance requirements, and implementation risk.
3. **Leadership and governance:** Stable executive sponsorship and clear accountability support complex transformation initiatives.
4. **Change management:** Employee training, communication, and organizational readiness are essential.
5. **Implementation strategy:** Phased deployment, coordinated testing, and risk management can improve implementation outcomes.
6. **Internal ownership:** Organizations must retain responsibility for strategic decisions even when working with external implementation partners.

## Conclusion

The contrasting SAP ERP experiences of Siemens Healthineers and Lidl demonstrate that successful enterprise transformation requires more than technological capability.

Strategic alignment, organizational readiness, effective governance, process standardization, and disciplined implementation are fundamental to realizing long-term ERP benefits.
